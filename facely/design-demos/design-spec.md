# Advanced Analysis redesign — shared direction specification

Facely’s Advanced Analysis is the interpretation layer after a face scan. Its audience is a mobile user holding the phone at roughly 25–40 cm, often with heightened sensitivity because the content concerns their own appearance. The screen therefore communicates analytical depth while remaining humane, private, and immediately understandable. The emotional tone is calm confidence: editorial rather than clinical, decisive rather than judgmental, premium without ornamental luxury. It should feel like a trusted coach distilled a complex model, not like a casino score, medical diagnosis, or social ranking.

The first viewport answers four questions in order: What is this? What matters most? Why? What can I do next? The title is Advanced Analysis. A compact summary reports strong, watch, and priority counts without icon-only tabs. The visual anchor is one high-contrast Focus now module showing the top two actionable priorities, their 0–100 scores, concise plain-language findings, and a route to detail. Below it, all metrics form a vertically scannable list grouped as Priorities, Watch, and Strong signals. Each row contains a meaningful category marker, metric label, short verdict, score, and disclosure affordance. The first priority is expanded enough to expose intelligence without a tap. One contextual action, Build my routine, appears only after the summary.

The detail state is one native-feeling bottom sheet, not a separate visual language. It contains metric name, category, 0–100 score, observed evidence, neutral reference range where appropriate, and one useful action. Progressive disclosure runs summary first, evidence second, educational reference last. Wording distinguishes controllable signals such as grooming or styling from mostly structural observations so the interface never implies that every facial trait needs correction.

Output is a self-contained HTML/CSS/JavaScript direction prototype at a 390×844 mobile viewport. Under Huashu’s weak-runtime mode, it consists of one complete layout direction plus two real palette/typography variants using the same architecture. Each is directly openable and clickable with category filters, expandable rows, a details sheet, and reduced-motion behavior. No external imagery is required for this direction stage.

Brand constraints are Facely’s near-black foundation, warm white surfaces, and signature lime #B4F34D. Lime is a focal accent rather than a wash. Coral is reserved for actionable warning and never becomes another brand. Body text is at least 14 px, annotations at least 12 px, normal text contrast meets 4.5:1, and targets are at least 44 px. Spacing uses 4/8 increments: 20 px page margins, 12–16 px component gaps, 24–32 px sectional separation, 16 px card padding, 24 px top-safe clearance, and 20 px plus device inset below bottom actions.

The visual motif is a signal brief: a long model output distilled into calm actionable signals. Its signature detail is a thin vertical signal line connecting priority rows, with one lime node marking current focus. On entry, the title stays static, the line draws once over 320 ms, and content resolves with an 80 ms stagger. Selection uses a 180–220 ms spring-like change using only opacity and transform. The detail sheet enters in 320 ms with velocity-aware dismissal. Scores never count from zero and motion never replays on tab focus. No pulsing, looping glow, full-card carousel, or per-frame score updates.

Three visual treatments preserve this hierarchy: A is recommended graphite editorial with one dark focus surface; B is warm paper-and-ink with serif display; C is a cool information atlas with tighter sans typography. All avoid gratuitous gradients, excessive rounded cards, tiny all-caps copy, decorative emojis, and abstract SVG faces.
